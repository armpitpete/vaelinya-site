# Liana and the Cave of Echoes

The covered passage repeated every voice.

Not clearly.

That would have been easier.

The stone roof curved low over the inventory route between the storehouse and the old kitchen. When one person spoke, the end of the sentence came back from the wall a moment later. When two people spoke, both echoes arrived on top of the next thing somebody said.

By the time four people were carrying boxes through it, the passage seemed to be having its own argument.

“Blue jars to shelf three.”

“Three,” said the wall.

“Wait, those are green—”

“Green,” said somewhere behind Liana.

“Move left.”

“Left.”

“Not you, the cart.”

“Cart.”

Pela stopped with a crate in both hands.

“I have no idea what anyone just told me.”

“Neither do I,” said Bram.

The passage returned a faint, late:

“Do I.”

Bram laughed.

Then he nearly walked into the side of the inventory cart.

Maer Tessa raised one hand.

“Stop the carrying.”

Everyone stopped.

The echoes did not.

For two breaths, scraps of the last instructions rolled along the stone.

Then the passage became quiet.

Liana noticed the difference.

Outside the covered route, people could talk over one another and still use faces, direction and distance to work out who was speaking to whom. Inside, the curved stone smeared those clues together.

Tessa looked at the crate labels.

“We have already put two jars on the wrong cart.”

Pela set her box down.

“So we need louder instructions.”

“No,” Bram said immediately.

“Why not?”

Bram cupped both hands around his mouth.

“BECAUSE LOUDER WILL BE—”

“LOUDER,” the passage shouted back.

Everyone winced.

Bram lowered his hands.

“Point made.”

Liana listened to the final echo fade.

“What if we wait for the room to finish?”

Pela looked around.

“The room is not talking.”

“It is repeating us.”

Tessa nodded. “Show me.”

Liana stood beside the first cart.

“Blue jars to shelf three.”

The words returned softly.

“Three.”

Liana waited.

The echo faded.

Then Tessa said, “Pela, take the blue jar box.”

“Box,” the wall repeated.

Pela waited too.

When the sound cleared, she lifted the correct box.

“That is very slow,” she said.

“Slow enough to understand?” Liana asked.

“Yes.”

They tried three more instructions.

One short instruction.

Wait for the echo to fade.

Next person speaks.

The work became easier to follow almost immediately.

Then Bram tried to improve it.

“We should have signals,” he said. “One hand means shelf one, two hands means shelf two—”

Liana shook her head.

“We already have shelf numbers.”

“But hands do not echo.”

Pela pointed out that people carrying boxes often did not have spare hands.

Tessa added that visitors and occasional helpers should not have to learn a private code just to move jars through one passage.

Bram lowered his proposed signalling system before it had become a system at all.

“Fine. Ordinary words with inconvenient breathing spaces.”

“Good title,” Pela said.

They resumed.

For several minutes it worked.

Then a cart wheel caught on a shallow join in the floor.

“Stop,” Tessa called.

Pela, at the same moment, said, “Lift the left side.”

Bram said, “Which left?”

The passage returned all three instructions in pieces.

“Stop.”

“Left side.”

“Left.”

Everyone froze.

Liana felt the confusion rise again.

Tessa waited until the echoes faded.

Then she said one thing.

“Nobody move the cart.”

Silence.

The passage repeated the last word and became quiet.

Tessa walked closer, looked at the caught wheel, and gave the next instruction only after she could see who needed it.

“Bram, lift your side two fingers.”

He did.

“Pela, pull straight.”

The wheel came free.

The pause had not solved the stuck cart.

It had only made enough space for the real instructions to arrive separately.

Halfway through the inventory, Liana's head began to feel full anyway.

Even spaced-out echoes were still echoes.

At the next doorway she stepped out into the open air and stood beside the wall.

Pela came after her with an empty crate.

“Are we stopping?”

“I am.”

“For how long?”

“I do not know yet.”

Pela nodded and carried the crate back inside.

Nobody stopped the whole inventory because Liana wanted a quieter moment.

Nobody told her she had to return on a schedule either.

She listened to ordinary courtyard sounds: a broom, a distant door, two people talking near the kitchen. They did not vanish, but they came from separate places instead of arriving doubled from stone.

After a few minutes, Liana went back in.

Tessa was giving an instruction.

Liana waited at the doorway until the echo finished before joining the route.

Bram noticed.

“You obeyed the cave.”

“No.”

“The cave demanded silence.”

“No.”

“What did it demand?”

“Nothing.”

Liana picked up a tray.

“We noticed that our words were harder to separate here. So we changed how we used them.”

Bram considered this.

“That is less dramatic.”

“Yes.”

By late afternoon the last boxes reached their shelves.

They had made fewer mistakes after changing the pace. One label still ended up on the wrong stack because Pela had simply read it incorrectly. The quiet spacing did not make people infallible.

They corrected the label.

Tessa wrote a temporary note for the next inventory group:

**IN COVERED PASSAGE: ONE SHORT INSTRUCTION, THEN LEAVE SPACE FOR THE ECHO TO FADE. IF YOU NEED QUIET, STEP OUT AND RETURN WHEN READY.**

Bram read it.

“No hand code.”

“No hand code,” Tessa confirmed.

“No sacred silence.”

“No sacred silence.”

Pela added, “No shouting at walls.”

Tessa looked at Bram.

“That one may deserve permanence.”

They carried the empty carts outside.

The moment they reached the courtyard, three conversations started at once.

Pela asked what was for supper.

Bram argued that shouting at walls had scientific value.

Tessa called across the yard for someone to return a key.

Liana answered Pela while another person laughed beside her.

Nobody waited for a stone echo that was no longer there.

The pauses had belonged to the passage and the work they were doing in it.

Outside, ordinary conversation returned exactly as noisy, overlapping and human as before.

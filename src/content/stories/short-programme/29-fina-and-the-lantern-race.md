# Fina and the Lantern Race

Nobody had said it was a race.

Fina had heard one anyway.

The village was rehearsing the route for an evening festival. In daylight, the children were using light wooden markers shaped like lanterns. Their job was simple: carry one marker from the weaving hall to the lower square, pass it on at each turn, and discover where the route became confusing.

The real festival would happen after sunset. People would carry lit lanterns between the hall, the pear court and the lower square while musicians and food carts moved by different routes. Maer Sori wanted the handover points tested before darkness made every mistake harder to see.

“Stay where the next person can find you,” Sori said.

Fina took the first marker and moved.

At the first corner she turned, lifted the wooden lantern above her head and waited.

Nobody came.

She walked back.

Sella, Bram and Pela were still at the narrow gate because Bram had stopped to retie a loose shoe strap.

“You went too fast,” Sella said.

“I reached the handover point.”

“We did not.”

Fina looked from the gate to the corner. The wall hid the place where she had been standing.

“Oh.”

They tried again.

This time Fina matched Bram's pace until the path opened into the pear court. Then she lengthened her stride without noticing.

“Fina.”

She stopped before Sella had to say anything else.

Bram caught up.

“I am not asking you to walk slowly forever,” he said. “I am asking you not to disappear.”

That was different.

Fina turned the wooden marker in her hands.

“What if I am not always first?”

Pela raised an eyebrow. “A historic proposal.”

They changed the rehearsal.

Fina carried the marker to the first turn and handed it to Sella. Sella took it through the pear court and passed it to Bram. Bram crossed the broad lower path and handed it to Pela.

Fina ran ahead only after her handover was finished, so she could stand at the next corner and check whether the marker remained visible.

That worked better.

Then Pela found a problem.

At the last turn, the marker disappeared behind a tall herb rack before the next handover place came into view.

“We need the turn earlier,” Pela said.

Fina checked from both directions.

Pela was right.

They moved the chalk mark.

Sori made them try again with a complication.

He asked two adults to wheel an empty food cart slowly through the pear court while the marker passed. Nothing dangerous happened, but the cart blocked the straight sightline for several breaths.

Fina, already at the far corner, raised the wooden lantern higher.

Sella could not see it.

Fina stepped sideways onto the edge of the path.

Still hidden.

She was about to run back when Bram called from behind the cart.

“Stay there. We know where you are meant to be.”

The cart cleared.

Sella saw the marker again.

They reached Fina without anyone running into the cart route.

“So I should not fix every delay by moving,” Fina said.

Sori nodded. “Sometimes the useful thing is to remain findable.”

Fina did not enjoy that sentence as much as she expected.

On the next rehearsal, Sella became the fastest walker because she had forgotten a note at the weaving hall and hurried back with it.

Fina almost called for her to slow down.

Then she noticed Bram could still see her.

The pace was not the rule.

Keeping the handover workable was the rule.

By midday they had a route with four clear exchanges. Sometimes Fina moved fastest. Sometimes she waited. Once Bram stopped entirely to answer a question from Sori, and the rehearsal still worked.

Then they tested what happened if somebody needed to stop between handovers.

Pela volunteered.

“You look too pleased about this,” Fina said.

Pela walked halfway across the lower path, stopped beside the wall and raised one hand.

“Pretend my ankle hurts.”

Fina immediately stepped toward her.

Sori said, “Who is responsible for the route?”

Fina stopped.

An adult helper who had been walking behind Pela moved to her side instead.

Pela pointed back toward Bram.

“The lantern should wait there until we know what is happening.”

Bram stayed at the previous handover.

Nobody lost the marker. Nobody made Pela keep moving to protect the pattern.

After a moment she announced that her imaginary ankle had made an excellent recovery.

“You are a terrible injured person,” Bram said.

“I was testing the system, not my acting.”

Fina laughed.

At the last daylight run, Fina handed the wooden lantern to Sella and stayed where she was.

Sella carried it away.

For a moment Fina had nothing to do.

Then Pela arrived from behind.

“Your turn is over,” Pela said.

“I know.”

“You look surprised.”

“I am practising.”

That evening, when the real lantern route began, the pear court looked completely different.

Warm lights hung beneath the eaves. People crossed between food tables. Music from the lower square arrived in broken pieces between buildings. The herb rack cast a dark shape across the place where the old chalk mark had been.

Sori walked near the group but did not direct every handover.

Fina received the first lit lantern at the weaving hall.

Her body wanted to move quickly the moment her fingers closed around the handle.

She did move quickly—through the open stretch where the next person could still see her.

At the narrow gate she slowed.

Bram was behind her, talking to a younger child who had become uncertain about the crowd.

Fina reached the first handover point and waited where the lantern remained visible.

It felt like being held by an invisible rope.

Then Bram looked up and nodded.

Fina handed the lantern to Sella.

Her part was done.

She moved ahead without it and checked the pear-court turn.

A group carrying bread trays had stopped near the planned line. Fina did not order them away. She asked Sori whether the handover could shift two paces toward the wall.

Sori checked the path.

“Yes.”

Fina showed Sella the new point before Sella reached the obstruction.

The route bent.

The festival continued.

Later, at the lower square, Bram handed the lantern back to Fina for the final short stretch.

“You can have another turn now,” he said.

Fina took it.

“Excellent system.”

Pela, behind them, said, “You said that this morning.”

“It has survived further testing.”

Fina walked quickly across the open square.

Halfway there she looked back.

Everyone could still see her.

So she kept moving.

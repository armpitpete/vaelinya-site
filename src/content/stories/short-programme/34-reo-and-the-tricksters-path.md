# Reo and the Trickster's Path

The workshop passage was closed.

A board across the doorway said so plainly.

**WET FLOOR — NO ENTRY.**

Reo read it, looked at the bundle of labels he was meant to deliver, and read it again in case the words had improved.

They had not.

Maer Siva stood beside the barrier with a mop bucket and two dry cloths.

“Wet floor,” she said. “Nobody through until it dries.”

Reo held up the labels.

“I have a delivery.”

“Yes.”

“To the other side.”

“Yes.”

“And the passage is between me and the other side.”

“You have understood the geography.”

Reo crouched to look beneath the barrier.

The stone floor gleamed from wall to wall. A jar of sizing mixture had split near the workshop door, and after the sticky liquid was cleaned away the whole passage had been washed. One section was still slick enough that Siva had left a broom lying across it as a second warning.

“What if I step only on the dry-looking stones?” Reo asked.

“No.”

“What if I take off my shoes?”

“No.”

“What if I crawl?”

“Reo.”

He stood.

“What if I become a delivery instead of a person?”

“No.”

“What if the labels go through without me?”

Siva paused.

“That is a different question.”

Reo stopped smiling.

The person who needed the labels, Maer Tovin, was working in the binding room on the other side of the passage. Reo had been thinking of his task as **walk labels to Tovin** because that was how he usually did it.

Siva asked, “What actually has to happen?”

“Tovin needs these before the binding bundles are closed.”

“Do you need to be standing beside him when that happens?”

“No.”

“Does he need anything else from you?”

Reo looked at the bundle. Each label already had its bundle number and storage mark.

“No.”

A narrow side hatch was set into the wall beside the closed passage. It was normally used for passing small tools between the two work rooms when both benches were busy.

Reo had walked past it hundreds of times without considering it a route.

It was not a route for people.

It did not need to be.

Siva checked the shelf on both sides of the hatch and opened the wooden panel.

“You may leave them here. I will tell Tovin they are waiting.”

Reo slid the bundle onto the dry shelf.

“That was disappointingly easy.”

“You wanted the barrier to lose?”

“I wanted my route to continue.”

“Your task continued.”

Reo looked at the closed passage.

That was annoyingly true.

He waited a moment.

“Can I knock on the hatch?”

“Why?”

“To make the delivery more dramatic.”

“No.”

Siva called through the adjoining workroom instead.

A few minutes later the hatch opened from the far side. The labels disappeared.

Then a folded note came back.

Reo opened it.

**LABELS RECEIVED. ALSO, YOU ARE NOT A DELIVERY. — TOVIN**

Reo borrowed Siva's pencil and wrote underneath:

**DISPUTED.**

He sent the note back through the hatch.

Siva laughed but did not move the barrier.

Reo's official job was finished.

His body did not agree.

He stood near the closed passage, watching people on the far side move past the doorway.

“I still want to go through.”

“I know,” Siva said.

“Why?”

“Because it is closed.”

“That is a poor reason.”

“It is a very Reo reason.”

He considered asking how long the floor would take to dry.

Then he realised the answer would not change what he had to do now.

“Do you need help on this side?”

Siva pointed at a stack of dry warning boards leaning against the wall.

“Take two to the north workshop. The long way.”

Reo groaned.

The north route did not involve the closed passage at all. It simply went around the exterior corridor.

He carried the boards.

On his way back, a younger helper named Jori stood at the barrier holding a shallow tray of metal clips.

“I need these in binding,” Jori said.

Reo pointed to the hatch.

“Small enough.”

Siva, still in charge of the closure, checked the tray first.

The clips could pass through safely without spilling.

Jori slid them across.

“Good shortcut,” he said.

Reo opened his mouth.

Then changed the sentence.

“Good transfer point.”

The distinction mattered. Nobody had found a hidden path around the rule. The people stayed on their own sides. Only the object moved through a place designed for objects.

A little later someone arrived with a broad drying frame.

“Through the hatch?” Jori suggested.

The frame was twice as wide as the opening.

Reo looked at it from three angles in case geometry had become negotiable.

It had not.

“Not this one.”

The worker checked with Siva. The frame was not urgent, so it stayed on the near side until the passage reopened.

Reo liked that less.

“Could take it outside and around,” he said.

“Could,” the worker replied. “Do not need to.”

Another task had a different answer.

No universal trick had appeared.

By mid-afternoon, the floor looked dry from the doorway.

Reo pointed this out immediately.

Siva did not reopen the passage.

She tested the stone near the spill with a clean cloth. It came away slightly damp.

“Still closed.”

“It looks dry.”

“That is why we test.”

Reo waited.

Ten minutes later Siva checked again.

Dry.

She asked another adult worker to walk the passage slowly in work shoes while everyone else stayed behind the barrier. The worker crossed, turned and came back.

No slip. No damp mark.

Only then did Siva remove the board.

“Open.”

Reo walked through normally.

Nothing dramatic happened.

Tovin was at the far bench attaching the last of Reo's labels.

“You found another path,” Tovin said.

“No.”

Tovin looked surprised.

“The hatch?”

“The labels found a hatch. I stayed where I was supposed to stay.”

Tovin nodded. “That is almost disappointingly responsible.”

“I was disappointed too.”

On the return journey, Reo used the passage because it was open.

The next week, he passed the same hatch while the floor was perfectly dry.

He did not send anything through it.

He had no task for it.

The trick had never been discovering a secret way that beat the barrier.

It had been noticing that **where Reo wanted to go** and **what the job actually required** were not always the same problem.

# The River of Flux

The water garden was crossed by a boardwalk that refused to stay the same shape for long.

Nothing magical moved it.

Gardeners did.

Shallow channels divided beds of reeds, mint, water flowers and young willow cuttings. Short board sections rested on low stone supports between them. Through the year, the gardeners shifted the boards as beds expanded, maintenance areas opened, and sections of bank became too soft for visitors.

Yesterday’s route could be perfectly correct and completely wrong by morning.

On workday morning, yesterday’s route ended beside a closed gate.

Fina stopped at the first junction.

“That was open yesterday.”

A red board now hung across it:

**REPLANTING — STAFF ROUTE ONLY**

Beyond the barrier, two gardeners were knee-deep in a reed bed.

The head gardener, Maer Osa, came along the path carrying a basket of pegs.

“We need a visitor route to the teaching terrace before noon,” she said. “And we need to keep our work routes usable.”

Fina held up the wooden START marker.

“Ready.”

Nira looked at the ground.

“Not yet.”

Beside the third board, the soil was dark and compressed with fresh footprints.

“That edge is still being used.”

Osa nodded.

“Reed crew crosses there.”

“So the board cannot become a visitor route even if it looks empty now.”

“Correct.”

Fina lowered START.

They walked the first section without moving anything.

Kaelen checked the board supports with Osa.

One section rocked when he shifted his weight.

“It carried people yesterday,” Fina said.

Kaelen crouched to inspect the stone beneath it.

“Yesterday’s bank was drier.”

Osa reset the support on firmer ground and tested it herself.

The route now curved away from the closed gate, but it pointed in what looked like the wrong direction.

Reo smiled.

“That one.”

“It goes away from the teaching terrace,” Fina said.

“For six paces.”

“It is still away.”

Reo followed it around the herb beds.

Two unused board sections connected to a wider path beyond them.

He looked back.

“Then it goes toward it for twenty.”

Fina joined him.

“Fine.”

“Did you just approve a detour?”

“I approve things that work.”

“Historic day.”

Lina was speaking with the gardeners at the reed bed.

She came back with three requirements.

“The reed crew needs repeated access here. The compost cart needs the east lane twice before lunch. The propagation team needs the small bridge near the quiet bench for one delivery.”

Valen found a slate.

“Those are not permanent route facts.”

“No,” Lina said. “They are today facts.”

He wrote the date and time at the top.

**CURRENT NEEDS — MORNING**

Then the three requirements.

Liana had walked to the quiet bench near the western water lilies.

The bench stood beneath a willow screen, just far enough from the busiest teaching beds to be useful to people who wanted a lower-traffic place.

She sat for a minute while the others argued about routes.

Then she returned.

“Reo’s visitor route passes close to the bench.”

Reo looked at the map.

“It does not touch it.”

“It turns the space beside it into a corridor.”

Osa frowned.

“We keep that area low-traffic during teaching days.”

Fina pointed to another movable board.

“Shift this one.”

Kaelen tested the proposed support.

“Too soft.”

Nira looked at the bank.

“There is firmer ground half a pace north.”

Osa moved the support there.

The visitor route curved away from the bench.

The detour grew longer.

Nobody died from the extra twelve steps.

Elara watched the plan take shape.

“Where does the compost cart cross?”

Valen pointed to the east lane.

“And the visitors?”

Reo traced the detour.

The two flows met at a narrow stone corner.

“Not at the same time,” Elara said.

Fina looked at Osa.

“When does the cart come?”

“Once in about half an hour. Again before lunch.”

Valen added the times.

They did not delete either route.

They changed timing.

Now they were ready for the first test.

Fina raised START.

She pushed an empty visitor trolley onto the route while Reo walked ahead.

At the second junction, the trolley fitted.

At the third, Kaelen checked the reset board before the wheels crossed.

At the western bend, Liana confirmed the quiet bench remained outside the traffic line.

At the east corner, Elara called HOLD because a gardener appeared with a basket.

Fina stopped.

The gardener crossed.

GO.

They reached the teaching terrace.

Fina parked the trolley.

“One route.”

Osa shook her head.

“One route in one condition.”

The condition changed ten minutes later.

A delivery arrived early.

Two workers brought long willow poles through the wide east lane.

The poles projected far beyond the handcart.

Elara saw them first.

“That flow is wider than the compost cart.”

The visitor route technically remained open.

It was not safely usable at the same time.

Valen changed the slate.

**EAST CORNER BLOCKED UNTIL WILLOW DELIVERY CLEARS.**

Fina lowered START.

Reo looked for another route.

The narrow bean path could bypass the east corner, but the trolley would not fit.

“It works for people without the trolley.”

Lina asked the teaching group what they actually needed transported.

“Three trays of samples and two water jugs.”

“Do they need the trolley?”

“No. It is easier.”

Fina looked at Reo.

“We can split the load.”

Kaelen checked the narrow path for footing.

Stable for people carrying light trays.

Liana checked where the path emerged.

It passed near the quiet bench but only for a short stretch and only for this temporary delivery.

“Okay if it stays temporary,” she said.

The group carried the materials through while the willow delivery occupied the wide lane.

The route had changed because the load had changed.

By midmorning, the reed crew moved farther along the channel.

Nira noticed the footprint pattern shift.

The work crossing they had protected was no longer being used.

A new one appeared two beds north.

Valen updated the slate.

Osa moved the STAFF ROUTE marker.

Fina pointed at the now-empty old crossing.

“Can visitors use it?”

Nira looked at the ground.

“Maybe after it dries.”

“It is empty.”

“Empty is not the same as firm.”

Kaelen tested the edge.

His boot sank.

“Not yet.”

Fina nodded.

The next failure came because Lina went to help a gardener carry labels.

While she was away, the children designed a very efficient afternoon route.

It avoided the quiet bench.

It avoided the compost lane.

It used firm boards.

It was shorter than the morning route.

Fina was delighted.

Then Lina returned.

“The propagation team needs the small bridge at two.”

Everyone stared at the map.

Their efficient visitor route crossed the bridge continuously.

Reo sighed.

“We optimised against incomplete needs.”

Valen added the propagation delivery.

Elara proposed a twenty-minute visitor pause around two o’clock instead of abandoning the route entirely.

Osa agreed.

They posted the time at both ends.

The route could be good and still unavailable sometimes.

Just before lunch, the compost cart arrived.

They held the visitor flow exactly as planned.

The cart crossed the east corner.

Then its rear wheel cracked a loose edging stone.

Kaelen stopped the reopening.

“The cart is gone,” Fina said.

“The edge is not safe yet.”

Osa reset the stone and added a temporary support.

Nira checked for new soil movement around it.

Only then did Fina raise START again.

By now the slate had more crossed-out lines than clean ones.

Valen refused to erase the old states completely.

“Why keep them?” Reo asked.

“So we can tell whether we are repeating a failed arrangement.”

He drew one thin line through each superseded note, leaving it readable.

At noon, a visitor arrived early with a cane.

The official teaching session had not started, but Osa asked whether they could test the route together.

The visitor agreed.

At the third board, she stopped.

“The step between these two is higher than the others.”

Kaelen had checked stability.

He had not checked the height difference from that user’s perspective.

Lina asked, “Would a handrail solve it?”

“Maybe. But the other route is flatter, isn’t it?”

Reo showed the longer detour.

The visitor tried it.

Better.

Fina looked at the route board.

“Do we make that the main route?”

“Why?” the visitor asked.

“Because it works better for you.”

“It works better for me. That does not tell you what every visitor needs.”

Lina smiled slightly.

They added both routes to the teaching map and described the differences.

**SHORTER — ONE HIGHER STEP.**

**LONGER — FLATTER SURFACE.**

No route became morally correct.

At two o’clock, the propagation team needed the small bridge.

Liana moved the temporary visitor marker away from the quiet bench while the delivery crossed.

Someone suggested using the bench as a waiting headquarters.

“No,” she said.

“Why? It is out of the way.”

“Because then every paused visitor gathers there and it stops being out of the way.”

They chose a broad sunny patch near the entrance instead.

That one decision prevented the accommodation for one need from consuming the accommodation for another.

By late afternoon, the seven of them—then eight, when Lina returned from another round of user questions—stood over the slate.

There was no single route drawn in thick permanent ink.

There were route options.

Conditions.

Times.

Loads.

Temporary closures.

Fina said, “This is harder than choosing a path.”

Reo said, “It is more interesting than choosing a path.”

Kaelen tapped one of the board supports with his boot.

“It is also more work.”

Elara looked at the garden flows.

“Because the routes are relationships between things that keep changing.”

Nira pointed toward the reed bed.

“Some changes are visible before they are written down.”

Valen held up the slate.

“And then they need to become visible to everyone else.”

Lina said, “Some changes are needs you cannot see until you ask.”

Liana looked at the quiet bench, still quiet despite a day of route changes.

“And some spaces work because traffic does not swallow them.”

Before the final visitor test, Osa changed one condition deliberately. She opened a watering gate that sent a shallow overflow across the apparently firm north approach.

Nira noticed the wet edge first and called the test off.

Reo found a bypass, but Kaelen rejected one board placement because its stone support had become slick. Lina checked whether the waiting visitors preferred to delay or take the longer dry route; they chose differently. Valen split the state board into **DRY ROUTE NOW** and **SHORT ROUTE AFTER WATERING**. Elara separated the people who chose to wait from the group already moving. Liana kept the waiting point away from the quiet bench. Fina restarted only the route whose conditions were actually ready.

Osa let them finish before saying, “That is why I changed the gate.”

Fina looked offended.

“You made the test harder.”

“I made the garden behave like a garden.”

The exercise showed that their route system did not depend on knowing every future change in advance. It depended on being able to notice, describe and revise when the change arrived.

Fina lifted START one final time.

“Afternoon visitor test.”

Nobody moved.

She looked around.

“What?”

Elara pointed toward the east lane.

A gardener was coming with a handcart.

Fina laughed and lowered the marker.

“Fine.”

They waited.

The cart passed.

Kaelen checked the corner.

Nira checked the wet edge.

Valen updated the state.

Lina confirmed the teaching group was ready.

Reo checked which route matched the trolley they were using.

Liana kept the western bench out of the flow.

Elara watched the crossing.

Then Fina raised START.

They moved.

By sunset, visitors had used five routes.

Two plans had been abandoned.

One destination had moved twice.

One route had been good for people but not trolleys.

Another had been good for a cane user and unnecessarily long for someone else.

The quiet western beds had stayed quiet.

The gardeners had kept every work route they needed.

Nobody had discovered the true path across the water garden.

There was no true path waiting to be found.

There were only paths that worked for particular people, loads, times and conditions—and a group willing to revise them when those conditions changed.

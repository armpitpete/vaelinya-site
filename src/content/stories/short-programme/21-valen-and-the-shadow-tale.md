# Valen and the Shadow Tale

By breakfast, the shadow had grown wings.

By midday, it had teeth.

Valen heard the tooth version beside the bread oven.

“It scraped the wall,” Pela said.

“It bit the shutter,” said Tor.

“You did not see it bite.”

“I saw the mark.”

“That is the same thing.”

“It is absolutely not the same thing,” Valen said.

They looked at him.

Pela folded her arms.

“You were not there.”

That stopped him.

He had been about to tell them what probably happened.

He knew the night wind had been strong.

He knew the lamp shed had a loose outer shutter.

He knew goats sometimes escaped the lower pen and wandered where goats had no business wandering.

A goat plus a shutter plus a lamp could make a very large shadow.

That was a good explanation.

It was also not Pela’s experience.

“What did you see?” Valen asked.

Pela stared into her cup.

“A shape on the wall.”

“How big?”

She spread both arms.

Tor said, “Bigger.”

Pela glared at him.

“He was behind me.”

“I could see the top.”

Valen waited.

Pela continued.

“The lamp was swinging. The shape moved across the wall. Then something hit the wood outside.”

“Once?”

“Twice.”

Tor said, “Three times.”

Pela said, “The third sound was him knocking over a stool.”

Tor looked offended.

“That was later.”

Valen took a piece of charcoal and the back of an old flour tally.

He wrote:

SHADOW ON WALL.
LAMP SWINGING.
TWO HITS OUTSIDE.
STOOL — PROBABLY TOR.

Tor leaned over.

“Remove probably.”

Valen crossed it out.

STOOL — TOR.

“That is rude.”

“It is accurate.”

Pela almost smiled.

Almost.

Valen added:

MARK ON SHUTTER.

Then he stopped.

“What did you do?”

Pela’s face changed.

“We ran.”

“Where?”

“To the herb room.”

“Good place?”

“Yes.”

“Could you get there again if you needed?”

She frowned.

“Yes.”

Valen wrote that too.

HERB ROOM ROUTE WORKED.

Tor looked at the list.

“That is a terrible story.”

“It is not a story yet.”

“It has a stool.”

“Many good stories have stools.”

Pela looked toward the door.

“I do not want to go past the lamp shed.”

That was the important part.

The shadow had happened once.

The fear was happening now.

Valen could tell a comforting story.

He could say: it was only a goat.

If he was wrong, comfort would become foolishness.

He could say: something unknown was there.

That was true, but it left the unknown as large as people wished to make it.

“Will you come look in daylight?” he asked.

Pela hesitated.

Tor said, “I will.”

Pela glared at him.

Valen said, “You do not have to.”

Pela took a breath.

“I will go as far as the herb-room corner.”

“Then that is where we stop first.”

The three of them walked after midday, when the sun stood high and shadows were short.

At the herb-room corner, Pela stopped.

Valen stopped too.

The lamp shed stood thirty paces away.

Its outer shutter hung crooked.

A dark scrape marked the lower board.

Tor pointed.

“Teeth.”

Valen walked closer.

Pela stayed where she was.

The scrape was broad, with pale wood showing beneath.

No tooth marks.

At the ground below it lay a broken branch.

Valen lifted the branch.

Its end matched the scrape.

“Branch hit the shutter,” Tor said.

“Maybe.”

“It fits.”

“It fits the mark.”

Tor sighed.

Valen looked at the lamp hook.

A length of cord hung from it.

The lantern itself had been taken inside.

Wind could swing it.

The branch outside could strike the shutter.

That explained two parts.

Then he found hoof marks in the soft ground.

Goat.

He smiled.

Pela called from the corner.

“What?”

“Goat prints.”

Her shoulders lowered a little.

“I told you,” Tor said.

Valen looked farther along the wall.

The hoof marks went past the shed.

They did not explain the shadow by themselves.

A goat could cast it.

So could a person.

So could the swinging branch.

He walked back to Pela.

“I think we have pieces.”

“Was it a goat?”

“Probably some of it.”

“Some?”

“The branch hit the shutter. A goat was here. The lamp could have swung. I cannot prove which thing made the shape you saw.”

Pela frowned.

Tor looked disappointed.

“That ending is worse than the stool.”

Valen sat on the low wall.

“What if the story is not ‘a creature came’?”

Pela said nothing.

“What if it is: the storm moved several things at once. A lamp swung. A branch struck wood. A goat got loose. You saw a big moving shape and did not know what it was, so you went somewhere safe.”

Pela looked at the shed.

Valen continued.

“We can fix the shutter. Cut the branch. Check the goat latch. Put a second lamp on the herb-room route.”

Tor said, “That sounds like chores.”

“Yes.”

“Terrible story.”

Pela smiled properly this time.

“Better ending.”

They did the chores.

The branch took longer to cut than expected.

The goat latch was not broken, only badly tied.

Tor insisted this proved the goat had planned everything.

No one agreed.

At dusk, they returned to the herb-room corner.

Pela stopped there again.

The new route lamp burned low beside the path.

The shutter sat straight.

Wind moved through the yard.

A shadow crossed the wall.

Pela caught Valen’s sleeve.

He did not say what it was.

They watched.

A drying cloth lifted on its line.

Its shadow rose long across the wall, then fell.

Pela let go of his sleeve.

“Cloth.”

“Yes.”

Another shadow moved.

Tor stepped into the lamplight and made two enormous ears with his hands.

Pela laughed.

“Do not.”

He added teeth.

Valen said, “Historically inaccurate.”

Tor made more teeth.

Pela laughed harder.

The yard had not become harmless.

Night still changed shapes.

Wind still moved things people could not see immediately.

But Pela now had more than one story available.

Something unknown had happened.

Some of it could be explained.

Some of it could be made safer.

If fear returned, the herb-room route still existed.

Valen liked stories that opened choices better than stories that closed every question.

On the way home, Tor said, “You never named the creature.”

“There may not have been one.”

“That is why you are bad at this.”

Pela walked between them.

“No,” she said. “That is why I can walk past the shed.”

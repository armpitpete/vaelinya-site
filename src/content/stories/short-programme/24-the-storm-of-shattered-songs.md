# The Storm of Shattered Songs

The rain arrived halfway through festival rehearsal.

Until then, the lower square had been noisy in an ordinary way.

Three groups were practising at once.

The drummers had six large skin drums mounted on wooden stands beneath the east arcade.

The reed players had music boards clipped to low frames near the fountain.

The dancers occupied the open centre of the square, where chalk marks showed the edges of their turning circles.

Every group had been told the same thing about bad weather:

If rain came, move under cover.

It sounded simple.

The first drops were large and widely spaced.

A drummer looked upward.

A dancer laughed.

Someone shouted, “Rain!”

Then the sky opened.

For three seconds, everyone did what seemed most urgent from where they stood.

The drummers lifted cases toward the north hall.

The reed players grabbed paper boards and ran for the kitchen passage.

The dancers moved toward the covered arcade.

Adults pulled cloth covers over props.

Fina saw the whole square begin to move and almost shouted, “Everyone north!”

She stopped because everyone was already moving.

In different directions.

The first drum case reached the north doorway sideways and blocked it.

Two dancers tried to squeeze around it.

A reed player came from the opposite direction carrying three loose boards above their head.

The boards struck the door frame.

Papers slid out.

Someone stepped backward into a drum stand.

The stand tipped.

Kaelen caught it before the drum hit the paving.

“Stop moving!” he shouted.

It was not the best instruction for a square full of people in rain.

But it stopped the nearest collision.

Fina took one breath.

“Not everyone. Just here.”

She held both hands up at the north door.

The people nearest her paused.

Rain hammered on the stones.

Nira stood under the edge of the arcade and watched the water itself.

“The west arcade is not dry,” she said.

Wind pushed the rain diagonally beneath its roof. A route that looked covered from the square was already wet halfway along.

She pointed north.

“The hall stays dry. The kitchen passage stays dry. The east arcade is dry near the wall but not at the edge.”

Reo looked at the blocked north doorway, then at the narrow kitchen passage.

“Drums cannot go through there.”

“No,” Kaelen said.

“People can.”

Reo ran the kitchen passage without carrying anything. It opened behind the north hall, bypassing the blocked doorway entirely.

“Paper and people through here.”

A reed player hesitated.

“Our cases?”

“Small cases fit. Not drum stands.”

Lina had already gone to the three group coordinators.

She did not ask, “What is your most important thing?”

That would have produced three answers competing to become the whole plan.

She asked each group what would be damaged first and what they could temporarily leave.

The dancers needed clear floor more than they needed their costume baskets.

The reed players needed the paper boards dry; most instruments were already in cases.

The drummers needed the exposed skins covered, but the heavy stands could survive rain for a short time.

Lina returned.

“Paper first through the kitchen passage. Drum skins covered where they are. Dancers need floor, but not yet.”

Valen found a white board under the hall eave.

He wrote the needs exactly as Lina had brought them.

**PAPER — DRY NOW.**

**DRUM SKINS — COVER NOW; STANDS CAN WAIT.**

**DANCERS — CLEAR FLOOR BEFORE ENTRY.**

Fina pointed at the first line.

“Then paper goes.”

Elara shook her head.

“The kitchen passage exits into the north hall. If the dancers enter the hall now, they meet the paper group from behind.”

Fina looked through the doorway.

Elara was right.

The problem was not only what moved first.

It was which movements overlapped.

Liana stood inside the north hall.

Rain on the roof made the room roar.

Everyone trying to give instructions simply became another layer of noise.

She found a spare board and drew two large marks.

**HOLD**

**GO**

She showed them to the group coordinators.

“Will these work at the door?”

The coordinators nodded.

Nobody had to hear a shouted command through the storm.

Nobody had to sing one shared signal.

The marks were ordinary, agreed and visible.

Kaelen and the stage keeper cleared two empty rack frames away from the north hall wall.

They marked a stable bay for the drum cases.

One frame had a cracked foot.

Kaelen set it aside rather than stacking equipment against it.

“Do not make the safe bay out of unsafe furniture.”

The keeper nodded.

Now they had enough pieces for a first plan.

Valen wrote it.

1. COVER DRUM SKINS IN PLACE.
2. PAPER AND SMALL CASES THROUGH KITCHEN PASSAGE.
3. HOLD.
4. HEAVY DRUM CASES TO NORTH BAY.
5. HOLD.
6. DANCERS INTO OPEN HALL.
7. STANDS LAST.

Fina read it.

“Too slow.”

“Which step can happen at the same time?” Valen asked.

Fina pointed to one and two.

Elara considered.

“Those flows do not cross.”

“Then together.”

Valen changed the board.

Fina took the GO sign at the square end.

Liana took HOLD at the hall door.

Nira watched the weather edge.

Kaelen stood by the case bay.

Reo took the kitchen route.

Lina stayed near the coordinators.

Elara positioned herself where she could see both flows.

Valen held the board where changes could be written immediately.

Fina raised GO.

The first movement began.

Reed players carried paper boards through the kitchen passage.

At the same time, drummers pulled waterproof covers over the exposed skins.

That part worked.

Then a gust pushed rain farther under the east arcade.

Nira raised her arm.

“Edge moved.”

One uncovered case that had been safe against the arcade wall was now getting wet.

Fina almost started the heavy-case movement immediately.

Kaelen pointed to the bay.

“Not ready.”

A stack of costume boxes had appeared in the marked space while everyone was watching the square.

The safe bay was no longer empty.

Valen crossed out **DRUM CASES TO NORTH BAY**.

“New bay?”

Reo looked around.

“The alcove behind the stairs.”

Kaelen tried the turn with an empty case trolley.

Too tight.

“Not for these.”

Lina spoke to the stage keeper.

“What can move from the first bay?”

“Costume boxes. They are dry and light.”

Fina and two adults moved those instead.

Now the drum bay existed again.

Elara waited until the paper group cleared the kitchen exit.

Then Liana turned HOLD to GO.

Heavy drum cases crossed the north doorway.

The first case went through.

The second wheel struck the threshold and jammed.

Someone behind pushed.

Kaelen said, “Stop.”

This time the word travelled through the people nearest the case, not across the whole square.

He crouched at the wheel.

The threshold had a raised metal edge.

“Do not force it. We change the approach.”

Reo looked at the angle.

“Come from the left instead of straight.”

They tried with the empty trolley.

It cleared.

Valen changed the route note.

Fina did not restart until Kaelen nodded.

The case passed.

The dancers were next.

They entered the hall and immediately occupied more space than the plan showed.

Their turning circle reached into the drum-case bay.

Elara clapped once.

Nobody heard.

Liana flipped the board to HOLD.

The next group stopped.

Lina spoke with the dance coordinator.

“How much floor do you need to rehearse safely?”

“Less than this if we mark a smaller practice circle. More than this if we use the full festival turns.”

“Which do you need during the storm?”

“Smaller is fine.”

Kaelen and the coordinator moved two floor markers.

The dancers practised a reduced pattern rather than claiming the entire hall.

The case bay remained usable.

The system was working.

Then the lights flickered.

The storm had darkened the north windows.

One reed player held up a damp sheet.

“We can save the paper, but we cannot read it here.”

The obvious answer was to move the reed group again.

Fina looked at the hall.

“Where?”

Nira pointed toward the kitchen side room.

“It has a window facing east. Less storm there.”

Reo checked the route.

Small cases fit.

Lina asked the reed coordinator whether splitting the group would ruin rehearsal.

“No. We can practise in two smaller groups.”

Elara said, “Then do not send them all at once through the dancer entrance.”

They staggered the move.

Valen updated the board.

The plan now barely resembled the first plan.

That was not failure.

It was current information.

For the next twenty minutes, the hall found a rhythm without becoming one rhythm.

Dancers practised quietly in one half.

Reed players worked in the side room.

Drummers checked cases beneath the dry part of the arcade.

The storm continued to change the edges.

Nira kept watching where water actually reached.

Fina started each movement when delay mattered, but she no longer treated movement itself as the solution.

Reo kept finding routes that served specific loads rather than one universal route.

Kaelen kept stands, bays and thresholds physically safe.

Lina kept asking the people doing the work what could change and what could not.

Valen kept the dependency sequence visible so revisions did not become rumours.

Elara kept good individual movements from colliding in time.

Liana kept the HOLD/GO intervals clear when sound failed as a coordination channel.

Then one mistake showed why all eight mattered.

Nira went to check the west arcade.

While she was gone, Fina saw the rain weaken and started moving the remaining drum stands beneath the arcade.

The rain looked lighter from the square.

It was still blowing hard along the west edge.

One stand became wet before Nira returned.

They covered it quickly.

Fina looked angry with herself.

“I saw less rain.”

“You saw the centre,” Nira said. “Not the edge.”

Fina nodded.

Later, Valen stepped away to carry paper.

A case route changed while he was gone.

Two adults followed different versions of the plan.

They met at the same doorway.

No one was hurt.

They still had to stop and reset.

After that, the board stayed with the board-holder.

Near the end, Liana handed the HOLD sign to someone who had not been part of the agreement.

They thought HOLD meant stop all work, not hold this doorway.

The dancers stopped unnecessarily.

Liana corrected the label:

**HOLD THIS FLOW**

**GO THIS FLOW**

The signal became more specific.

The rain eased after an hour.

Sunlight did not return, but the square stopped shining with running water.

Nobody rushed outside.

Nira checked the paving.

Kaelen checked the drum stands.

Lina asked each coordinator what needed to move back first.

Valen started a new return sequence instead of reversing the old one automatically.

Elara separated the exits.

Reo found that the kitchen passage was now blocked by food trays, so the route changed again.

Liana moved the HOLD/GO station to the south doorway.

Fina started the first return.

By sunset, the square was active again.

The drummers had not become dancers.

The dancers had not adopted the reed players’ timing.

The reed players had not learned a shared chant that solved bad weather.

Nothing had fused into one perfect festival song.

Mara, one of the organisers, looked at the white board covered in crossed-out steps.

“It is ugly.”

Valen smiled.

“It is accurate.”

A dancer pointed to the layers of changes.

“Can we keep it for tomorrow?”

“Not as instructions,” Valen said. “Tomorrow may be different.”

“As a record, then.”

“That we can keep.”

Fina looked across the square.

Before the rain, three groups had been practising three different things.

During the storm, pressure had made each group’s urgency collide with everyone else’s.

The solution had not been to make them care about the same thing.

It had been to coordinate what differed.

Elara heard the drummers begin again beneath the arcade.

A reed line started from the side room.

The dancers counted their steps in the hall.

Three rehearsals.

Three tempos.

Three different needs.

They overlapped without destroying one another.

The storm had shattered the assumption that everyone could simply “move under cover.”

What replaced it was not one song.

It was a way for different work to remain different and still survive the same pressure together.

# Kaelen and the Earthquake Tree

The cups rattled before anyone felt the ground move.

Kaelen was stacking them after the midday meal when six clay cups began tapping against one another on the shelf.

Tik-tik-tik-tik.

Kaelen put a hand on the shelf.

The wood trembled.

Then the courtyard shifted under their feet.

Not far.

Not enough to throw anyone down.

Enough.

A stool scraped sideways. Water jumped out of a basin. Somewhere beyond the wall, a dog barked once and then would not stop.

For one moment, nobody in the courtyard moved.

Kaelen looked toward the old tree beside the north wall.

They had looked toward it hundreds of times.

The tree had a trunk wider than two people standing shoulder to shoulder. Its roots lifted the paving stones in slow ridges. On crowded days, people used it as a meeting point.

Wait by the old tree.

Leave the basket by the old tree.

If you lose us, find the old tree.

Kaelen knew exactly where it was even with their eyes closed.

The ground moved again.

Familiar felt like safe.

Kaelen took one step toward the tree.

“Open area!” called Steward Mira.

The words cut across the courtyard.

“Everyone to the marked open area. Away from walls and branches.”

Kaelen stopped.

A white circle had been painted on the broad centre stones during the spring safety practice.

Kaelen knew that too.

The tree was familiar.

The steward’s instruction was for this situation.

Kaelen changed direction.

“Sera,” they called.

Sera was standing near the meal table with both hands pressed to her ears.

Her face had gone pale.

“I cannot,” she said.

Kaelen went to her, but not back toward the wall.

“The open circle is this way.”

The ground gave another small shiver.

Sera looked at the tree.

“I want to go there.”

“So did I.”

“Is the tree safer?”

Kaelen looked at the broad branches above the north wall.

“I do not know.”

Another adult, Pell, was already guiding two younger children across the courtyard.

“Follow Mira,” Kaelen said. “That part we know.”

They did not take Sera’s arm.

Sera hated being grabbed when she was frightened.

Kaelen walked half a step ahead instead, slowly enough that Sera could match them.

One stone.

Then another.

At the white circle, Steward Mira was counting people.

“Seven, eight, nine—Kaelen, Sera. Good. Stay here.”

“Is it over?” Sera asked.

Mira shook her head.

“The shaking has stopped for now. We are waiting while the buildings are checked.”

Sera’s breathing sped up.

Kaelen wanted to say, It is fine.

The words rose automatically.

They swallowed them.

A cracked tile slid from the roof edge and shattered beside the wall.

Nobody was near it.

Sera stared.

“It is not fine.”

“No,” Kaelen said.

That was true.

Mira heard and crouched a short distance away.

“We are in the safer waiting place,” she said. “The roof and walls are being checked. We do not go back until the check is finished.”

Sera nodded once.

Then she looked at Kaelen.

“What do we do?”

Kaelen looked down at the painted circle.

Its edge was chipped in three places. Someone had spilled soup across one side at midday, and the wet patch was slowly drying.

Small things.

Known things.

“We stay inside the mark,” Kaelen said.

Sera put both feet well inside it.

Kaelen did the same.

The dog beyond the wall kept barking.

A baby began crying near the gate.

Two adults spoke quietly about the storehouse.

Kaelen’s own legs had started to shake.

At first they thought it was the ground again.

Then they realised it was them.

Sera noticed.

“You are shaking.”

“Yes.”

“I thought you were calm.”

“I am standing here.”

“That is not the same.”

Kaelen almost smiled.

“No.”

Mira passed them a water cup from the emergency basket.

Kaelen took it with both hands because one hand was not steady enough.

Sera watched.

“Can I have one?”

Mira gave her a cup too.

They drank.

The courtyard stayed still.

A few minutes passed.

Then a low rumble came through the stones.

“Aftershock,” Mira called. “Stay clear.”

This movement was shorter than the first.

The old tree shook hard.

Its leaves flashed pale and dark.

A dead branch, high above the wall, snapped.

It fell into the place where Kaelen had first wanted to stand.

The branch hit the paving stones with a heavy wooden crack.

Everyone in the white circle went silent.

Sera looked at Kaelen.

Kaelen looked at the branch.

They felt cold all the way down their back.

If Mira had not called—

Kaelen stopped the thought.

It was too easy to turn one frightening possibility into a whole imagined ending.

Instead they looked at what was actually there.

The branch had fallen.

The open area had remained clear.

The steward had known the safety plan.

Kaelen’s familiar place had not been the right place for this moment.

Mira counted everyone again.

When the shaking stopped, two building stewards checked the walls from a distance. Another adult went around by the south gate to inspect the tree without walking under it.

Nobody rushed.

That was harder than Kaelen expected.

Waiting while other people checked things felt like doing nothing.

They wanted a task.

They wanted to hold something steady.

Sera sat on the ground inside the circle.

Kaelen sat too.

“You can lean on me if you want,” Sera said.

Kaelen blinked.

Usually Kaelen was the one people leaned on.

Their legs were still trembling.

“All right.”

They sat shoulder to shoulder.

Not because Sera needed Kaelen to be strong.

Not because Kaelen needed Sera to be strong.

Because both of them were there.

After a while, Pell came back from the south side.

“The north wall has a new crack,” he reported to Mira. “The courtyard rooms stay closed until the mason checks them. South gate is clear.”

Mira nodded.

“We will move to the lower field in pairs.”

Kaelen stood.

Their knees felt uncertain.

Sera waited.

“You first?” Kaelen asked.

“Together.”

They walked through the south gate.

In the lower field, people spread blankets on the grass. Someone brought bread. Someone else took names again.

The ground did not move for the rest of the afternoon.

By evening, the mason had checked the main buildings.

Two rooms remained closed.

The courtyard reopened only for collecting essential things, with adults controlling the route.

Kaelen went back the next day.

The broken branch had been cut into manageable pieces and stacked away from the wall.

The old tree still stood.

Sunlight moved across the bark.

Its trunk was as broad as ever.

Kaelen put a hand against it.

The bark felt rough and solid.

For a moment they wondered if touching it was somehow wrong now.

Then Mara came through the gate carrying a basket.

“Meet me by the old tree after lunch?” she asked.

Kaelen looked up.

The tree had not become useless because it had not been safe during the tremor.

It was still easy to find.

Still shady.

Still a good ordinary meeting point, once the tree itself had been checked.

“Yes,” Kaelen said.

Mara went on.

Kaelen stayed another moment.

Near the base of the trunk, Steward Mira had tied a small inspection tag to one low branch.

CHECKED AFTER TREMOR — DEAD LIMB REMOVED.

Kaelen read it twice.

The tree could be familiar.

The safety plan could change what familiar meant in an emergency.

Both things could be true at once.

A breeze moved the checked branches overhead.

Kaelen stepped out from beneath them and continued across the courtyard.

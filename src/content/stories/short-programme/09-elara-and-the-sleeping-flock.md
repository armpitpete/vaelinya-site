# Elara and the Sleeping Flock

The Path Goats were awake.

They simply would not come out.

Eleven of them stood inside the low shelter above the growing terraces, packed shoulder to shoulder in the dimness. Their ears were up. Their eyes were open. One chewed slowly.

From a distance, they looked calm.

Elara did not trust distance with that kind of judgement.

Maer Seli stood outside the shelter holding a feed basket.

“They have been in there since dawn.”

Elara looked at the yard.

A storm had passed during the night. No tree had fallen. No wall had failed. The roof of the goat shelter was intact.

But beside the gate lay a long sheet of woven rain-cover that had torn loose from its frame. One corner still caught the wind.

It lifted.

Slapped the ground.

Lifted.

Slapped.

Every goat inside the shelter flinched at once.

Elara felt her own shoulders tighten.

“That,” she said.

Maer Seli nodded. “We tied it twice. It came loose twice.”

“Can we move it?”

“We need it for the seed beds later.”

“I mean now.”

Together they folded the loose sheet and weighted it with two stones beside the wall.

The yard became quiet.

Maer Seli shook the feed basket.

The goats did not move.

A small brown goat stepped one hoof forward.

Then back.

Maer Seli sighed. “They know the noise is gone.”

Elara watched the brown goat.

“Do they?”

“It is gone.”

“That was not what I asked.”

Maer Seli looked at her.

Then at the shelter.

“All right.”

They waited.

Elara sat on the low wall beside the gate.

She did not sing.

She did not speak softly at the goats as if soft words could explain a storm.

She did not carry the brown goat outside to show the others it was safe.

The goat had legs.

It also had the right to use them backwards.

After a while, Maer Seli scattered a little feed just inside the doorway.

The nearest goats ate it.

She scattered more at the threshold.

The brown goat approached.

One hoof outside.

A gust moved through the yard.

A wooden scoop hanging from a peg struck the wall.

Clack.

The brown goat jumped backwards.

The whole flock compressed deeper into the shelter.

Maer Seli closed her eyes.

Elara got up and took the scoop down.

“There are more noises than the sheet.”

“Yes.”

They walked the yard slowly.

A bucket handle rattled against its rim.

A gate hook tapped in the wind.

Two empty carrying frames leaned together and knocked when the gusts reached them.

None was dangerous.

That did not mean none mattered.

They secured the bucket handle with cord, laid the carrying frames flat and wrapped cloth around the gate hook.

Then Elara stood in the middle of the yard.

“Now?” Maer Seli asked.

Elara listened.

Wind through grass.

Water running in the drainage channel.

A Root-Hen calling from below the terraces.

People talking near the tool shed.

Ordinary sound.

“I do not know,” Elara said.

Maer Seli laughed once. “Helpful.”

“It is quieter. That is what I know.”

She moved the feed basket to the grass beyond the gate.

Nothing happened.

Ten breaths.

Twenty.

The goats stayed inside.

A boy named Pell came up the path carrying fresh bedding.

“Still sleeping?”

“They are not sleeping,” Elara said.

Pell peered into the shelter.

“They look sleepy.”

The brown goat stamped once.

Pell reconsidered.

“Maybe not.”

He set the bedding down.

“We need them out to change that.”

Maer Seli nodded.

Pell stepped towards the doorway.

Elara said, “What are you going to do?”

“Lead them out.”

“How?”

“Walk behind them.”

The goats watched him.

Pell stopped.

“Oh.”

The shelter had one main doorway. A person walking in behind the flock would make the only obvious route out feel narrower, not wider.

He stepped away again.

“What if we open the side panel?” he asked.

Maer Seli looked at the shelter wall.

A low woven panel on one side could be removed in warm weather for airflow.

“It opens into the herb strip.”

“Is that bad?”

“Not if we put a temporary fence around it.”

They did.

The new opening was wider than the doorway and faced away from the place where the rain-cover had been striking the ground.

Elara sat down again.

The goats now had two exits.

For several minutes they used neither.

Then the brown goat approached the side opening.

It stretched its neck out.

Sniffed.

Stepped onto the grass.

Nobody cheered.

Elara was glad.

The goat took three steps, turned sharply and went back inside.

Pell whispered, “Why?”

Elara shrugged.

The goat came out again.

This time it stayed beside the opening.

Another goat followed halfway, then reversed.

A third stepped outside, ate one mouthful of grass and stood still.

The flock did not suddenly become brave.

It became less certain that outside was impossible.

That was slower.

It was also theirs.

Maer Seli moved the feed basket closer to the side opening but did not shake it.

Two more goats came out.

Then a gust crossed the yard.

The folded rain-cover lifted at one edge despite the stones.

It did not slap.

But it moved.

Three goats ran back into the shelter.

The brown goat stayed outside, body tense.

Elara watched it.

“Should we move the cover farther away?” Pell asked.

“Probably.”

Maer Seli looked at the seed beds. “We can store it behind the tool wall until the wind drops.”

They carried it away.

When they returned, the brown goat was grazing.

Elara did not call that victory.

By midday, seven goats had chosen the yard. Four remained inside.

The bedding still needed changing.

Maer Seli looked at the four.

“We can wait longer.”

Pell said, “Or change half today.”

Elara looked at him.

He pointed to the empty side of the shelter.

“We do not need the whole floor clear at once.”

So they changed the bedding in sections.

They moved no goat by force.

When one stood in the part Pell needed, he worked somewhere else.

When another stepped outside, they cleaned that space.

By the time the sun moved past the roof edge, ten goats were out.

One old grey goat remained in the far corner.

Maer Seli sat on the threshold.

“She may stay there all afternoon.”

Elara nodded.

“Then she stays.”

“We cannot leave old bedding forever.”

“No.”

Maer Seli smiled. “You always make the second sentence necessary.”

They made a plan to return before evening. If the grey goat still would not leave, they could move the clean bedding around her and try again later, unless health or safety made waiting impossible.

The flock grazed in the yard.

The brown goat wandered back into the shelter once, looked around, then came out through the main doorway.

Pell noticed.

“It used the old door.”

“Yes.”

“So it is fixed.”

Elara watched the goat choose a patch of grass near the wall.

“No.”

Pell waited.

Elara added, “It used the old door.”

That was enough to know.

The storm was over.

The fear did not have to end on the same schedule.

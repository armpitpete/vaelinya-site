# Valen and the Endless Thread

Valen found three notices pinned in three different rooms.

Each said that a plan had changed.

That was enough to make a story begin in their head.

The first notice was outside the weaving hall:

**EVENING PATTERN MEETING MOVED TO THE SMALL ROOM.**

The second was beside the communal kitchen:

**BREAD TABLES NEEDED ONE HOUR EARLIER.**

The third hung at the south workshop:

**WOOD-CUTTING SESSION MOVED TO TOMORROW.**

Valen read all three twice.

“Festival preparation,” they said.

Pela, carrying a bundle of clean cloths, stopped beside them.

“What about it?”

“All these changes.”

“What all these changes?”

Valen pointed toward the weaving hall, then the kitchen, then vaguely toward the workshop.

“They are pushing one another around.”

Pela looked at the first notice.

“Do you know that?”

“Not yet.”

Valen copied the three notices onto a large scrap board in the archive workroom. They wrote each room at one corner and drew charcoal lines between them.

Weaving hall to kitchen.

Kitchen to workshop.

Workshop back to weaving hall.

The shape was satisfying.

That should have made Valen suspicious.

Instead it made them lean closer.

“If the bread tables moved earlier because the weaving meeting needed the large room, and wood-cutting moved because the kitchen needed the courtyard—”

Pela put down the cloths.

“You have used 'because' twice without asking anyone.”

Valen looked at the board.

“Then we ask.”

The weaving hall was easiest.

Maer Tessa was moving pattern boards into the small room.

“Why did the meeting move?” Valen asked.

“Because the large tables are being borrowed by the kitchen for festival bread.”

Valen smiled.

One line had earned itself.

At the kitchen, Maer Delen confirmed the other half. The bread tables were needed earlier because the dough schedule had changed, and the weaving hall had agreed to release the large tables sooner.

“So those notices are connected,” Valen said.

“Yes,” Delen replied.

Pela tapped Valen's notebook.

“Two notices.”

Valen ignored the correction with dignity.

They went to the south workshop.

A carpenter named Sova was tying a cloth over a stack of cut boards.

“The wood-cutting session moved because the courtyard is needed for festival bread?” Valen asked.

Sova stared at them.

“No.”

“The kitchen needs more space.”

“Perhaps it does.”

“And the weaving tables moved.”

“I heard.”

“So why did this move?”

Sova pointed toward the cutting frame.

One of its iron guides had cracked.

“We are waiting for the smith.”

Valen looked at the broken guide.

The third line on the board disappeared in their mind before they even returned to erase it.

Pela was less merciful.

“Separate things are allowed to stay separate.”

“I know.”

“You drew a triangle.”

“It was provisional.”

“You admired it.”

Valen erased the workshop line.

Two notices stayed connected.

One stood alone.

They wrote beside it:

**REASON: TOOL REPAIR. NO EVIDENCE OF FESTIVAL CONNECTION.**

That should have ended the matter.

It did not.

Valen kept thinking about the workshop notice.

The smith was busy because of festival work, perhaps.

Or the iron guide had cracked because the workshop had rushed to prepare festival boards.

Or the session moved to tomorrow because tomorrow's courtyard schedule was different.

Any of those could reconnect the lonely notice.

Valen went back to Sova.

“Was the guide damaged during festival work?”

“No. It cracked last week.”

“Is the smith delayed by festival work?”

“I do not know.”

“Did tomorrow's schedule affect the choice of new time?”

“Yes. Tomorrow was free.”

Valen brightened.

“That is a connection.”

Sova shook her head.

“To the ordinary schedule board. Not necessarily to your festival chain.”

Pela, who had followed because she was now entertained, laughed.

Valen wrote another note.

They asked the smith.

The smith's delay came from repairing a water-pump bracket, not festival work.

They asked the workshop scheduler.

Tomorrow had been chosen because two workers were available then. One of those workers happened to be free because the festival had cancelled a different task.

Valen felt the thread pull tight again.

“There.”

Pela frowned.

“There what?”

“One worker is available because of a festival cancellation. That influenced tomorrow.”

“Influenced,” Pela said. “Is that the same as 'the workshop moved because of the festival'?”

Valen opened their mouth.

The answer they wanted was yes.

The answer that survived the question was no.

They returned to the board.

Under the workshop notice Valen wrote:

**Tomorrow's staffing includes one festival-related availability. Primary cause of cancellation remains broken guide.**

The sentence was uglier than the triangle.

It was also better.

After lunch, Valen came back to the archive and found themself staring at the three notices again.

They thought of another person they could ask.

Then another.

Then another.

Pela sat opposite them and ate an apple.

“What would change if one of them said yes?”

Valen looked up.

“What?”

“What would you actually learn that matters?”

“That there is another connection.”

“Would it change why the workshop session moved?”

“No.”

“Would it change what anyone should do?”

“No.”

“Would it correct something uncertain on the board?”

Valen looked at their notes.

Not really.

They had stopped learning anything new. They were only looking for a way to keep the long story alive.

Valen put down the charcoal.

“That is annoying.”

Pela bit the apple.

“Yes.”

They left the board alone until after lunch had become afternoon.

When Valen returned, they redrew it.

Two notices stayed connected by one dark line.

The workshop notice stayed separate, with a smaller note about the staffing overlap.

Underneath they wrote:

**OTHER CONNECTIONS — UNKNOWN. ADD ONLY IF THEY CHANGE THE ACCOUNT.**

The next day another plan changed.

A notice appeared beside the garden room:

**SEED-SORTING MOVED TO WEST TABLES.**

Valen felt the thread beginning again.

This time they did not reach for charcoal.

They found Maer Iven and asked one question first.

“Why did this move?”

“The east table has a cracked leg.”

Valen waited.

Iven waited too.

“Anything to do with the festival?” Valen asked eventually.

“No.”

Valen returned to the board.

Pela watched them from the doorway.

“Well?”

Valen pinned the garden notice on its own.

“No line.”

Pela looked almost disappointed.

“You are improving.”

“I reserve the right to connect it later if evidence earns one.”

“That sounds dangerous.”

“It is the whole point.”

Valen stepped back.

The board no longer told one elegant story.

It showed several things: a real chain between weaving and bread tables, a broken workshop tool, a small staffing overlap, a cracked garden table, and blank space where future connections might or might not appear.

The thread had not ended.

It had become something Valen could cut, knot, leave loose and pick up again without pretending every loose end belonged to the same cloth.

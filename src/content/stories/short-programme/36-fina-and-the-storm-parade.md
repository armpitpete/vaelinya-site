# Fina and the Storm Parade

The rain arrived while everyone was still arguing about whether it would.

One moment the craft yard was full of paper decorations, baskets of chalk and strips of bright cloth waiting to be tied into festival chains.

The next, the sky cracked open.

Rain hammered the roof edges and bounced from the paving stones. A gust lifted three paper circles from the nearest table and pasted one against the wall.

Maer Sori clapped twice and pointed toward the hall.

“Inside, please. Leave the tables. Adults will bring the rest.”

Most people moved.

Fina moved fastest.

She had crossed half the covered route before she realised three younger children had not left the yard.

Nemi, Jori and Pell stood beneath the narrow roof edge with Sori. The hall was only a short distance away, but hard rain fell between the covered places. Water was already running along one side of the paving.

Fina turned.

Her first impulse was to run back, take someone's hand and make the whole stalled group move.

She stopped before doing it.

Sori was beside them.

“Can I help start them?” Fina called.

Sori nodded. “Show the route. Do not rush them.”

Fina came back along the covered edge.

Nemi shook her head.

“It is too loud.”

The rain struck the roof above them like hundreds of small thrown stones.

Pell stared at the water crossing the open gap.

Jori had both hands over his ears.

Fina looked at Sori.

“What if I go first and stop at the chalk post?”

Sori looked at the children instead.

“You can watch first,” she said.

Nobody moved.

Fina walked to the painted post halfway along the route. She stayed under the deep eave, raised both arms where they could see her, and waited.

Sori raised one hand in answer.

Fina continued to the hall door and raised both arms again.

Nemi watched.

“Again,” she said.

Fina wanted to say, **You have seen it now.**

Instead she went back.

She did the route again.

Post.

Arms up.

Door.

Arms up.

On the third pass, Pell noticed that Fina never stepped into the deepest water running beside the path.

“You stay near the wall,” he said.

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Sori told us that side drains better.”

Fina almost added, **Come and see.**

She did not.

Nemi asked, “Can you do it slower?”

Fina blinked.

“Yes.”

The fourth demonstration felt ridiculous to her body. She could have crossed the whole distance before counting to ten.

She walked at half speed anyway.

At the chalk post she stopped long enough for Nemi to look at the path behind her.

At the hall door she turned and waited.

Sori asked the group, “Anybody want to try as far as the post?”

Pell shook his head.

Jori shook his head without uncovering his ears.

Nemi said, “With you.”

Sori walked with her.

Fina stayed at the hall door.

Every part of her wanted to run back and join them.

She made herself remain visible instead.

Nemi and Sori reached the post.

Nemi stopped.

Rain sheeted across the yard beyond them.

She looked at Fina.

Fina lifted both arms again.

Nemi came the rest of the way.

Inside the hall, she accepted a dry cloth from another adult and stood beside the wall.

Pell came next, but only after asking Sori to repeat where the water was shallowest.

Jori waited longest.

The rain did not become quieter.

Nobody persuaded him that it was not loud.

Sori showed him a pair of padded ear coverings from the craft supply box and asked whether he wanted them.

He nodded.

With them on, he watched Fina do the route one more time.

Then he crossed with Sori.

The route was no longer unknown.

That had been enough to make trying possible.

Inside, someone called the repeated movement “Fina's storm parade.”

“I was not leading a parade,” Fina objected.

Nemi, wrapped in a dry cloth, said, “You kept waving.”

Sori asked, “What did the waving mean?”

Fina started to answer.

Nemi answered first.

“It meant she got there.”

Fina nodded.

Not **come now**.

Not **you are safe because I say so**.

It meant only what they had used it to mean: Fina had reached the next visible point.

A louder burst of rain hit the roof.

Pell looked toward the door.

“Are the paper decorations ruined?”

Fina had already turned toward the yard.

Sori touched her shoulder.

“Not your job.”

“Some are getting wet.”

“Yes.”

“I can move fast.”

“Yes.”

Fina waited for the part where those facts became permission.

It did not arrive.

Adults in rain capes were already securing the loose materials. One table had begun to slide on the wet stones, and two adults moved it together rather than sending children back into the yard.

Fina stood in the hall doorway.

Stopping was harder than crossing the rain.

Nemi appeared beside her.

“You look like you are going to run through the wall.”

“I am practising not helping.”

“That sounds strange.”

“It feels strange.”

Nemi looked at the adults outside.

“They have it.”

“I know.”

The two of them watched until the last paper basket came under cover.

Then Sori closed the outer door most of the way, leaving enough gap for the adults to return.

The hall had become noisy too, but differently. People were shaking water from sleeves, moving chairs and asking where to put rescued materials.

Fina found a new task.

She asked Nemi whether she wanted to unfold a fresh sheet of paper at the side table.

Nemi nodded.

Jori joined them after taking off one ear covering. Pell sorted the dry chalk from the damp pieces.

No one called that part a parade.

Half an hour later the rain eased.

Sori opened the door fully.

Water still ran along the yard edge, but the hard roar had softened to a steady patter.

Nemi stood at the threshold.

“Can we go back?”

Sori checked the yard with the other adults first.

“Not yet. One table is still unstable.”

Fina waited for Nemi's reaction.

Nemi shrugged and went back to the paper.

The route that had been useful for coming in did not grant automatic permission to go out again.

Later, when the yard reopened, Fina crossed it at ordinary speed.

At the chalk post she stopped without meaning to.

The paint was wet and dark beneath the eave.

Nemi came up behind her.

“No parade?”

Fina looked at the clear route ahead.

“No need.”

They walked on together.

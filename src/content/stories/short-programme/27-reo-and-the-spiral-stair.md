# Reo and the Spiral Stair

The archive courtyard had a path everyone called the Spiral Stair even though it was completely flat.

Its pale stones curled around the central herb bed in five broad loops before reaching the record room door.

Reo disliked this immediately.

“The door is there,” he said, pointing across the courtyard.

Maer Siva nodded.

“And the path is here.”

“That is an unreasonable amount of path.”

“It keeps carts away from the planted beds.”

That was a reason, which was inconvenient.

Reo was carrying a bundle of blank labels under one arm. Pela had a box of binding cord. They had been sent from the archive store to the record room, a distance that looked like twenty paces and behaved like sixty.

Reo followed the spiral.

On the first loop he passed a bed of mint.

On the second, he passed the same mint from another side.

On the third, he stopped.

“I have seen this plant enough.”

“The mint is delighted,” Pela said.

Reo noticed a narrow paved cross-path between two herb beds. A small board beside it read:

**FOOT TRAFFIC — WHEN OPEN.**

“Does that reach the record room?” he asked Siva.

“Yes.”

“May I test it?”

Siva checked that the garden workers had left the cross-path clear.

“Yes.”

They walked it together.

It cut across two loops and joined the main route near the record room.

Reo reached the door before Pela, who had deliberately stayed on the spiral.

“Much better,” he said.

“For people on foot, when it is open,” Siva said.

Reo looked back at Pela approaching around the final curve.

“Still better.”

Pela arrived.

“You were carrying labels. I was carrying a box.”

“The box fits.”

“This box fits.”

Reo recognised the tone. It meant Pela had stored a future argument.

The next morning she used it.

They were sent back to the record room with a wider crate containing stiff document boards.

Reo turned toward the cross-path.

The entrance was open.

The space between the herb beds was not wide enough for the crate unless they tilted it almost upright.

Pela stopped.

“Your brilliant route has failed.”

“It has dimensions.”

“That sounds less brilliant.”

“It sounds more accurate.”

They used the spiral.

Its curves were annoying, but the broad path gave them room to carry the crate level without brushing the plants.

Halfway around, a small handcart came toward them from the opposite direction. The cart driver waited at a widened passing place while Reo and Pela moved by.

Reo looked at the path differently.

The spiral was not long because someone had failed to notice the door.

It had been made to carry things through the courtyard without making the herb beds into obstacles.

He still preferred the cross-path when it suited him.

That afternoon the shortcut was closed completely while gardeners moved trays across it.

A red cord blocked the entrance beneath the sign.

Reo stopped.

Pela, beside him, said, “Your brilliant route has failed again.”

“It has conditions.”

“That sounds less brilliant.”

“You already used that joke.”

“It remains accurate.”

Reo looked across the open courtyard. He could see the record room door. He could also see the gardeners carrying wet trays through the cross-path.

For one second he considered walking around the red cord through the grass.

Siva, who was nowhere near him, seemed to appear in his imagination saying, **the planted beds are not spare path**.

Reo sighed and joined the spiral.

Later in the week, the cross-path opened again. Reo asked Pela to walk it from the opposite direction while he used the main path.

They met at the record room.

“Same destination,” Pela said.

“Different route.”

“Today.”

Reo grinned.

Then they tried another comparison.

Maer Siva lent them an empty archive cart. Pela pushed it along the spiral. Reo walked the cross-path carrying nothing.

He arrived first.

Pela arrived with the cart, stopped beside him and said, “Congratulations on transporting no records very efficiently.”

Reo leaned on the wall.

“This experiment was biased.”

“Toward reality?”

“Unfairly.”

They tested the route in light rain too. The cross-path remained open, but one paving stone near the middle became slippery with leaf fragments. A gardener swept it clear and left a warning cone until it dried.

The spiral's stones, more exposed to wind, dried faster.

Reo added another condition to his mental list.

Foot or cart.

Wide load or narrow load.

Open or closed.

Dry enough or temporarily slippery.

None of those conditions made one path secretly true and the other false.

They only made different routes workable at different times.

Siva found Reo studying the courtyard route board later that day.

“What are you changing?” she asked.

“I have not decided.”

“That is unusually cautious.”

“The board says the cross-path exists, but people keep walking to it before discovering whether it is open.”

“True.”

“What if we put the open-or-closed marker here, at the first spiral junction?”

Siva considered the suggestion.

That would let people choose before walking halfway across the courtyard.

“Good,” she said.

They made a small reversible card:

**CROSS-PATH OPEN FOR FOOT TRAFFIC**

On the reverse:

**CROSS-PATH CLOSED — USE SPIRAL**

Garden workers could flip it when their work changed the route.

Reo wanted to add **FAST WAY** to the open side.

Pela objected.

“Fast for whom?”

“People with legs.”

“People with legs carrying a wide crate?”

Reo crossed out the words before Siva could say anything.

They settled on:

**CROSS-PATH: CHECK OPEN BEFORE USE.**

The spiral stayed.

So did the shortcut.

Over the next few days Reo used both.

He took the cross-path with a handful of labels.

He took the spiral with a cart.

Once he chose the spiral simply because he wanted to finish a conversation with Pela without squeezing past people on the narrower route.

Once Pela took the cross-path while Reo chose the spiral because the mint smelled good after rain.

She waited at the record room door.

“You voluntarily used the unreasonable amount of path.”

“I had a reason.”

“So did the courtyard.”

Reo looked back at the pale stones curling around the herb bed.

He still thought five loops were excessive.

But he had stopped needing one of the routes to defeat the other.

The useful question was no longer, **Which path is correct?**

It was, **What are you carrying, what is open, and what works now?**
